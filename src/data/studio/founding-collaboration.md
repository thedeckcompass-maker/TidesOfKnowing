## What is Deck Creator Studio?

### A connected workspace for developing original card-based tools and creative decks

Deck Creator Studio is a specialised creative development and publishing environment within Tides of Knowing.

It is designed for coaches, facilitators, educators, practitioners, writers, artists and other creators developing their own card-based work: reflection and prompt decks, learning resources, conversation tools, coaching and personal-development decks, tarot, oracle and other original systems.

Its purpose is to bring the many interconnected parts of deck creation into one cohesive working environment.

Rather than managing card meanings in one document, artwork in another, research across multiple notebooks and guidebook drafts in separate files, the Studio is designed to help you develop these elements as parts of one connected project.

**The Studio provides structure without prescribing your creative vision.**

The broader system encompasses the following areas.

### For coaches: a tool clients can take with them

If you are a personal coach developing your own methodology, a deck can give clients a tangible take-home tool for working with your ideas between sessions. Your approach does not have to be finished or fully named before you begin.

Our editorial work can help you turn that approach into a coherent set of reflection prompts, practical exercises and questions, with clear guidance on when and how to use them.

The cards might help clients revisit a conversation, practise an exercise, prepare for their next session or try applying your work in everyday life. You can consider what someone needs to understand when you are not beside them to explain it, and how the cards and accompanying guide can make those next steps clear.

Together, we can consider how the deck fits your existing work, what clients can use independently and where your direct guidance remains important. A take-home deck accompanies your coaching; it does not replace the personal relationship or promise a particular result.

You might begin with a resource for individual clients, a shared tool for groups or workshops, or an idea you eventually want to offer more widely. The purpose, structure and wording can develop as you work; you do not need a complete methodology or a tarot or oracle framework to start.

### Purpose and creative identity

Define the purpose of your deck, its intended audience, underlying approach and the experience you want its questions, activities, imagery or meanings to offer.

Explore what makes your project meaningful and how its central ideas can remain consistent throughout the deck.

### Card structure and relationships

Develop your card list, content, themes, categories and relationships. A deck can support a sequence of exercises, a set of learning themes, an open collection of prompts or a symbolic framework.

Whether your cards follow a sequence, explore a set of themes, support a professional conversation or invite open-ended reflection, the objective is a deck whose individual cards work together as a coherent whole.

### Guidebook writing and editorial development

Develop your accompanying guidebook alongside the cards.

A good companion does more than describe individual cards. It communicates the purpose of the deck and helps someone understand how to use its questions, exercises, imagery or meanings.

The Studio is designed to support the creation of a consistent, well-structured manuscript.

### Artwork and visual planning

Organise visual concepts, reference material, creative briefs and artwork requirements.

You may be creating your own illustrations, commissioning an artist, using photography, exploring digital techniques or still determining the visual direction.

Completed artwork is not a requirement for beginning your project.

### Creative journaling and research

Record intuitive insights, research, visual inspiration, reflections and evolving ideas.

Creative projects rarely unfold in a perfectly linear sequence. The Studio is intended to accommodate that reality while helping you retain connections between your discoveries and the developing deck.

### Copyright and publication preparation

Maintain records of your creative work and explore the questions surrounding authorship, permissions, attribution and intellectual property.

The wider Studio framework also addresses artwork collaboration, production planning, printing, publication and crowdfunding considerations.

Not every creator has the same destination. Some want a personal or gift edition. Others want a tool for coaching, teaching, workshops or client work. Others intend to publish independently or launch commercially.

Deck Creator Studio is designed to accommodate these different ambitions.

**The platform is already in development and has working capabilities.** Additional features form part of its continuing roadmap. Founding collaborators will work with the existing Studio and contribute feedback that helps determine which further capabilities will provide the greatest benefit.

## Why I created the Studio

### The Aotearoa Bird Oracle

My own creative journey provided the inspiration for Deck Creator Studio.

I am currently developing the Aotearoa Bird Oracle, a substantial channelled and researched work that has evolved into a 78-card oracle and tarot hybrid.

It brings together the birds of Aotearoa New Zealand, their symbolism and a broader system of intuitive interpretation connected with traditional tarot.

As the project developed, I found myself managing an increasingly complex collection of card interpretations, artwork, research, creative notes and evolving ideas.

I had numerous open files, illustrations and written fragments, all contributing to a project whose meaning was continuing to deepen.

The challenge was not a lack of inspiration.

It was finding a way to hold everything together without losing the creative and intuitive nature of the work.

I needed more than somewhere to store files.

I needed a system that could accommodate the unfolding creative process while providing the editorial structure required to bring the project towards publication.

That need became Deck Creator Studio.

The Aotearoa Bird Oracle is being developed within the Studio.

Through creating the platform for my own work, I began to recognise how valuable it could become for other deck creators facing similar challenges.

## What I bring to your project

### Professional editorial experience, with an understanding of creative and symbolic work

My background combines more than 40 years of experience as a tarot Reader with 30 years working as a professional journalist and editor.

These disciplines complement each other.

Intuitive work involves recognising patterns, understanding symbolism, perceiving relationships and discovering meaning.

Editorial work involves examining complex information, identifying its underlying structure and communicating it with clarity, consistency and purpose.

For a coach, educator or other creator, the task is to turn what you know and notice into a coherent experience someone else can use. Creative insight and editorial structure can support that work, whether or not the deck uses tarot or oracle traditions.

A compelling idea is only the beginning.

The deeper challenge is developing that idea into a coherent system of cards, imagery and writing that expresses its meaning while remaining accessible to the people who will eventually use it.

That is where my particular combination of skills can be especially valuable.

### Editorial guidance that respects your creative vision

I can help you examine your deck's purpose, strengthen its structure, develop clear language and recognise the connections that give the work depth.

I can also help you identify inconsistencies, unnecessary repetition, missing elements and areas where an idea requires further development.

Just as importantly, I can help you recognise when it is time to stop exploring possibilities, make a decision and move forward.

**My role is not to create your deck for you or impose my interpretation on your work.**

It is to help you recognise what is already there, develop what is missing and bring the different elements together into a coherent creative project.

## How the collaboration works

### Stage 1: Studio onboarding and a setup session with me

**Onboarding arranged with you individually**

We will begin with two preliminary live online sessions: first, onboarding into Deck Creator Studio; second, a setup session with me. Neither session carries a payment obligation.

These will be practical working sessions in which I introduce you to Deck Creator Studio, explain how the system operates and guide you through establishing your own project.

We will arrange your introductory sessions with your location, time zone and availability in mind.

The objective is to get you actively working inside the Studio, rather than simply watching a demonstration.

### Stage 2: Explore before making a financial commitment

**Decide after both preliminary sessions**

Between and after these preliminary sessions, you can begin using the Studio with your own project.

During this introductory period, you can explore its capabilities, begin developing your deck and reach out with questions as they arise.

Your project questions and feedback are part of our private working relationship. Any optional community participation is separate; your project material is not shared with others automatically.

After your Studio onboarding and setup session with me, you can choose whether to proceed into the paid founding collaboration, based on fit and value.

**No payment is required before that decision.**

By this stage, I want you to have enough experience to judge whether the Studio and my professional support will be valuable to you.

If you decide not to continue, there is no financial obligation.

### Stage 3: Active editorial collaboration

**Approximately two to three months in total**

For those who continue, we will move into a structured period of editorial and creative development.

We will agree a schedule for regular online working sessions, based on your project and our availability.

These will be purposeful editorial working sessions, not simply general conversations about creativity.

We will examine your progress, discuss challenges, review areas requiring attention and identify the decisions needed to move your project forward.

Our work may include:

- Refining your deck's philosophy, purpose and interpretive framework.
- Reviewing card structures, meanings and symbolism.
- Developing the relationship between written material and artwork.
- Strengthening guidebook organisation, language and editorial consistency.
- Examining creative and practical obstacles.
- Discussing copyright, permissions and publication considerations.
- Reviewing progress against agreed milestones.
- Identifying the next steps required to move closer to completion.

The precise emphasis will depend on your project and the priorities we agree.

### Individual support between meetings

I also expect to be personally involved between our scheduled sessions.

There will be opportunities to reach out when you encounter a difficulty or need guidance on an issue holding up your project.

This individual attention is an important part of the founding collaboration.

I want to understand your creative journey, not simply hear about it once a week.

At the same time, our meetings and individual support will remain focused on helping you progress rather than turning into open-ended conversations.

### Stage 4: Continue developing your deck

The end of our supported collaboration does not mean your project has to be finished.

Your founding place includes one year (twelve months) of Deck Creator Studio platform access, beginning with your first onboarding. There are no additional platform subscription costs during that year.

Your included platform access continues after the approximately two-to-three-month supported collaboration ends, for the remainder of that year. The year of access does not extend the period of personal support.

Your project may require additional writing, artwork, editing or production preparation after our direct work together concludes.

You will have that additional time within the Studio to continue developing it.

I do not expect everyone to have a printed deck by the end of our collaboration.

**I do expect genuine commitment to making progress and ultimately completing the work.**

## Who should apply?

### You do not need a finished deck

I welcome people with a genuine intention to develop a card-based tool or creative deck: for personal use, professional practice, learning, reflection or publication.

You do not need to be a professional artist, an established Reader or a published author.

You do need a meaningful creative idea, a willingness to develop it and an understanding that completing a deck takes sustained effort.

You may be someone who:

- Is a coach or professional who wants to turn their approach into a practical tool for clients or groups.
- Is developing reflection prompts, conversation cards, learning activities or a resource for workshops.
- Has been developing a deck for some time but struggles to organise the work.
- Has written card meanings but needs to create a coherent guidebook.
- Has artwork or visual concepts but needs help developing the interpretive system.
- Has a strong intuitive or philosophical vision but needs editorial structure.
- Has started a substantial project and become overwhelmed by the number of decisions involved.

You can be at the very beginning of your creative journey, with only an idea, or you may already have artwork, written material or a substantial deck under development. How far you have progressed will not make you eligible or ineligible.

What matters in deciding whether to work together is that you genuinely want support bringing your vision to life, are willing to put effort into that work and feel comfortable providing honest, constructive feedback about what you want or need from the Studio. You do not have to praise features that do not work for you. Candid feedback is part of the value of our collaboration.

This is a working collaboration, not a passive learning experience.

I will ask participants to:

- Commit regular time to developing their projects.
- Attend our agreed editorial meetings wherever possible.
- Work independently between sessions.
- Take part constructively in our agreed working sessions.

You will not be expected to share unpublished material publicly.

I recognise that creative projects can contain personal, confidential or culturally sensitive material, and we will agree appropriate boundaries for our work together.

What matters most is that you are ready to take your project seriously.
